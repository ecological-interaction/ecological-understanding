# Ecological Understanding

Ecological Understanding is an interdisciplinary framework for investigating, developing, teaching and applying knowledge of ecological systems. It brings together scientific observation, geographic information, situated knowledge, systems thinking and computational methods to support research and ecologically informed practice. Through research questions, methodological resources, practical tutorials and structured educational programmes, it supports researchers, designers and practitioners in understanding ecological relationships and translating that understanding into place-specific, regenerative approaches.

## Objectives and principles

The framework connects ecological inquiry with research methods, learning and place-specific practice. Resources can be adapted to different audiences and contexts while preserving attribution, transparent methods and critical evaluation.

Scientific evidence, situated accounts and design judgements retain their distinct provenance and limits. Claims of ecological improvement require appropriate evidence.

## Four framework functions

| Function | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Research | Frame questions and investigate ecological relationships. |
| Methods & Tools | Explain how to observe, analyse and interpret. |
| Education & Programmes | Organise resources into learning pathways. |
| Applied Practice | Inform and evaluate design and ecological interventions. |

Every domain supports all four functions; individual resources may serve several.

## Components, modules and classification

Components are reusable resources such as research activities, practical procedures or worked examples. Teaching modules combine activities around objectives and outputs; programmes assemble them into a coherent sequence.

| Dimension | Classification |
|---|---|
| Function | Research; Methods & Tools; Education & Programmes; Applied Practice |
| Domain | One or more of EU-01–EU-10 |
| Content type | Research; How-to; Teaching Module; Full Programme; Application |
| Pathway | Introductory; Technical; Intermediate; Advanced; Research; Applied |
| Difficulty | 1 Foundation; 2 Basic; 3 Intermediate; 4 Advanced; 5 Specialist / Research |

These dimensions are independent. Each resource records its audience, questions or objectives, tasks, outputs, prerequisites, relationships, sources and review status. Card indicators describe subject classification and approach; they do not change this metadata contract.

## Courses and programmes

Researchers, designers and educators can combine resources across domains for a particular audience, place or question. Assemblies specify objectives, sequence, prerequisites and outputs, checking source versions, permissions and local suitability. Reuse preserves the original resource and its attribution.

## Relationship to Ecological Interaction

The framework draws on [Ecological Information](https://ecological-interaction.github.io/information/), including EIRKB, EII, GISDS and OSDS, and independently maintained computation tools. Source knowledge bases remain authoritative; the developing DRM supports federation identities, relationships and governance, while the developing IRKB concerns interdisciplinary synthesis. These are architectural relationships, not claims of completed integrations.
