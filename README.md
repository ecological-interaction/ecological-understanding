# Ecological Understanding

Ecological Understanding is an interdisciplinary framework for investigating, developing, teaching and applying knowledge of ecological systems. It brings together scientific observation, geographic information, situated knowledge, systems thinking and computational methods to support research and ecologically informed practice. Through research questions, methodological resources, practical tutorials and structured educational programmes, it supports researchers, designers and practitioners in understanding ecological relationships and translating that understanding into place-specific, regenerative approaches.

## Objectives and principles

The framework supports inquiry into ecological relationships and the translation of knowledge into research methods, educational resources and applied practice. It aims to make the connections between observation, interpretation and action explicit, while enabling resources to be adapted to different places, institutions and audiences.

Ecological Understanding is an overarching framework with a developing catalogue of reusable resources. Short practical activities, research components and complete programmes can coexist within it. Its thematic domains provide multiple entry points rather than a compulsory sequence of ten teaching stages.

Its principles are interdisciplinary inquiry, attention to place, transparent methods, source attribution and critical evaluation. Scientific observations, situated accounts, theoretical arguments and design judgements should retain their distinct provenance and interpretation limits. Claims about ecological improvement require evidence appropriate to their context.

## Four framework functions

| Function | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Research | Pose questions, compare explanations and develop investigations into ecological relationships. |
| Methods & Tools | Explain how to observe, document, analyse and interpret ecological information. |
| Education & Programmes | Organise learning activities, teaching modules and educational pathways. |
| Applied Practice | Use and evaluate ecological understanding in design, technology choice and situated interventions. |

All four functions are available across every thematic domain. A resource may support more than one function: a field investigation can serve research, teach a method and inform a design decision.

## Components, modules and classification

A component is a reusable unit such as a research question, practical procedure, worked example or assessment activity. A teaching module combines related activities around explicit objectives and outputs. A full programme assembles resources into a coherent learning or research experience.

| Classification | What it describes | Available values |
|---|---|---|
| Framework function | The purpose served | Research; Methods & Tools; Education & Programmes; Applied Practice |
| Thematic domain | The subjects addressed | One or more of EU-01–EU-10 |
| Content type | The form of the resource | Research; How-to; Teaching Module; Full Programme; Application |
| Educational or research pathway | The trajectory or mode of engagement | Introductory; Technical; Intermediate; Advanced; Research; Applied |
| Difficulty | The knowledge and skills required | Level 1 — Foundation; Level 2 — Basic; Level 3 — Intermediate; Level 4 — Advanced; Level 5 — Specialist / Research |

Pathways and difficulty are separate. An Applied resource might introduce a simple field activity or require specialist modelling. A Technical pathway may include both basic and advanced work. Prerequisites explain the specific preparation needed rather than relying on a difficulty label alone.

Each resource should identify its research questions or learning objectives, practical tasks, expected outputs, prerequisites, related resources, source references and intended audiences. Development and review status should make clear whether readers are seeing an outline, a draft or a reviewed resource.

## Courses and programmes

Educators and researchers can assemble resources into short courses, focused technical sequences, research pathways or full programmes. An assembly specifies its audience, objectives, ordered activities, prerequisites and expected outputs. It can bring together resources from several domains and adapt them to a local question without producing competing copies of the original materials.

For example, a future place-based investigation might combine observation design, spatial source selection, indicator interpretation and technology comparison. This illustrates a possible assembly, not an existing course. Reuse requires checking source versions, permissions, preparation and the suitability of activities for the intended place and participants.

## Using the framework

- Researchers can use the domains to frame questions, identify methods and connect evidence across disciplines.
- Designers and practitioners can connect site knowledge and ecological indicators to design requirements, technology choices and monitored interventions.
- Educators can select components by audience, pathway and difficulty, then assemble and assess a coherent programme.

## Relationship to the Ecological Interaction infrastructure

Ecological Understanding develops research and educational resources within the wider [Ecological Interaction](https://ecological-interaction.github.io/) ecosystem. It draws on [Ecological Information](https://ecological-interaction.github.io/information/) for references, indicators and source discovery, including EIRKB, EII, GISDS and OSDS, and can explain and reuse independently maintained computation tools.

The source knowledge bases remain responsible for their records and classifications. The developing DRM provides federation identities, relationships and governance; interdisciplinary synthesis is associated with the developing IRKB. Educational resources can teach and apply synthesis without assuming responsibility for either infrastructure. These relationships describe the intended architecture rather than completed technical integrations.
