# EU-05 — Systems Science & Ecological Relationships

This domain uses systems thinking to investigate ecological relationships, feedbacks and change. It supports comparison of interacting biological, environmental and social processes while making system boundaries and modelling assumptions explicit.

This foundational page identifies opportunities for research and resource development. The activities below are proposals, not a claim that completed modules already exist. Research, Methods & Tools, Education & Programmes and Applied Practice are all supported within this domain.

## Scope and principal subjects

- System boundaries, stocks, flows, feedbacks and delays
- Networks, emergence, nonlinear dynamics and complex adaptive systems
- Resilience, thresholds, disturbance and cross-scale relationships
- Social–ecological systems, institutions and coupled resource use

## Relevant research questions

- Which feedbacks sustain or destabilise a particular ecological condition?
- How do processes operating at different scales interact?
- What evidence distinguishes a causal relationship from an association in a system model?

## Potential methods and how-to components

- Construct a system-boundary statement and causal-loop diagram.
- Build a simple stock-and-flow model with stated assumptions.
- Compare network representations and test sensitivity to missing relationships.

## Teaching module opportunities

- Systems reasoning for ecological research and design.
- Feedbacks, resilience and social–ecological change.

Teaching resources can be adapted to different pathways and difficulty levels. Each adaptation needs explicit prerequisites, learning objectives, tasks and expected outputs; the domain itself has no fixed difficulty.

## Applied research and design opportunities

- Map interacting water, soil, vegetation and management processes at a study site.
- Investigate potential unintended consequences of a proposed intervention.
