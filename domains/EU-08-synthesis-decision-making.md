# EU-08 — Ecological Synthesis & Decision-Making

This domain brings different forms of evidence into comparative interpretation and decision-making. It investigates how research findings, ecological indicators and situated knowledge can inform explicit choices while preserving uncertainty, disagreement and the provenance of supporting claims.

This foundational page identifies opportunities for research and resource development. The activities below are proposals, not a claim that completed modules already exist. Research, Methods & Tools, Education & Programmes and Applied Practice are all supported within this domain.

## Scope and principal subjects

- Evidence synthesis, comparative analysis and interdisciplinary interpretation
- Scenarios, spatial decision support and multi-criteria evaluation
- Trade-offs, values, uncertainty, participation and decision accountability
- Knowledge graphs, place analogues and transfer of findings between contexts

## Relevant research questions

- How can heterogeneous evidence inform a decision without obscuring its different strengths and limits?
- How sensitive is a preferred option to assumptions, weights and uncertain observations?
- When can a result from one place inform another, and what must be re-evaluated locally?

## Potential methods and how-to components

- Build an evidence-to-claim matrix for a stated decision.
- Compare intervention options using explicit criteria and sensitivity analysis.
- Develop scenarios that distinguish observations, assumptions and value judgements.

## Teaching module opportunities

- From ecological evidence to reasoned decisions.
- Synthesis across disciplines, places and forms of knowledge.

Teaching resources can be adapted to different pathways and difficulty levels. Each adaptation needs explicit prerequisites, learning objectives, tasks and expected outputs; the domain itself has no fixed difficulty.

## Applied research and design opportunities

- Prepare a documented options appraisal for a land-management intervention.
- Develop a research-backed decision brief with traceable evidence and unresolved questions.
