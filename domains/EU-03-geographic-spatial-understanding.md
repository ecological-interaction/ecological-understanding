# EU-03 — Geographic & Spatial Understanding

This domain develops spatial reasoning for ecological investigation. It considers how cartography, geographic information systems and remote sensing describe places, reveal patterns and support comparisons across scales and observation periods.

This foundational page identifies opportunities for research and resource development. The activities below are proposals, not a claim that completed modules already exist. Research, Methods & Tools, Education & Programmes and Applied Practice are all supported within this domain.

## Scope and principal subjects

- Coordinate reference systems, projections, extent and resolution
- Raster, vector, terrain and Earth-observation information
- Spatial sampling, classification, overlays and change analysis
- Map communication, spatial uncertainty and place-based interpretation

## Relevant research questions

- At what spatial and temporal resolution can a proposed relationship be investigated?
- How do map boundaries and classification choices affect descriptions of a landscape?
- When are two places sufficiently comparable for a spatial analogue to be informative?

## Potential methods and how-to components

- Select geographic sources and record product-level metadata.
- Prepare and align spatial layers with documented reference systems.
- Compare land-cover observations and communicate uncertainty on a map.

## Teaching module opportunities

- GIS and remote sensing: concepts and theory, methods and tools, application.
- Reading terrain, land use and ecological patterns across scales.

Teaching resources can be adapted to different pathways and difficulty levels. Each adaptation needs explicit prerequisites, learning objectives, tasks and expected outputs; the domain itself has no fixed difficulty.

## Applied research and design opportunities

- Prepare a spatial evidence base for a landscape design brief.
- Map a study site and evaluate candidate monitoring locations.
