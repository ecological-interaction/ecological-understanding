# EU-07 — Ecological Computation & Intelligence

This domain investigates computational methods for processing ecological observations and developing analytical tools. It distinguishes operations such as image transformation from higher-level interpretation, prediction and decision support, while treating reproducibility and validation as integral to both.

This foundational page identifies opportunities for research and resource development. The activities below are proposals, not a claim that completed modules already exist. Research, Methods & Tools, Education & Programmes and Applied Practice are all supported within this domain.

## Scope and principal subjects

- Image processing, vegetation indices, spatial analysis and bioinformatics
- Data preparation, software environments, reproducible workflows and versioning
- Statistical learning, object detection, prediction and knowledge representation
- Model evaluation, transfer limits, interpretability and computational uncertainty

## Relevant research questions

- Which transformations are needed to make an observation suitable for analysis?
- How well does a model transfer between places, instruments or seasons?
- Which errors arise from the data, algorithm, assumptions or display choices?

## Potential methods and how-to components

- Run and document an RGB-to-index processing workflow.
- Separate numerical results from false-colour display settings.
- Compare model outputs with an independent reference and record failure cases.

## Teaching module opportunities

- Reproducible ecological computation from inputs to outputs.
- From processing tools to ecological interpretation and intelligence.

Teaching resources can be adapted to different pathways and difficulty levels. Each adaptation needs explicit prerequisites, learning objectives, tasks and expected outputs; the domain itself has no fixed difficulty.

## Applied research and design opportunities

- Prototype an analysis pipeline for a field observation programme.
- Evaluate whether a computational tool is suitable for an operational design or management task.
