# EU-10 — Regenerative Systems & Practice

This domain investigates interventions intended to improve ecological functioning and the relationships that sustain it. It connects restoration, agriculture and territorial practice with baselines, explicit objectives, monitoring and adaptive learning. Regenerative outcomes are treated as claims to evaluate in context.

This foundational page identifies opportunities for research and resource development. The activities below are proposals, not a claim that completed modules already exist. Research, Methods & Tools, Education & Programmes and Applied Practice are all supported within this domain.

## Scope and principal subjects

- Restoration, agroecology, soil, water and habitat management
- Baselines, reference conditions, intervention design and counterfactuals
- Long-term monitoring, adaptive management and distributed learning
- Ecological and social outcomes, trade-offs, durability and unintended effects

## Relevant research questions

- What constitutes improvement relative to a documented baseline and whose objectives are represented?
- How can observed change be distinguished from seasonal variability or external pressures?
- Which practices remain effective over time and under different ecological conditions?

## Potential methods and how-to components

- Define intervention objectives, a baseline and an evaluation plan.
- Design a monitored field trial with appropriate comparison conditions.
- Document an adaptive-management cycle and decisions made from new evidence.

## Teaching module opportunities

- Regenerative practice as a monitored ecological intervention.
- Place-based experimentation and learning across research sites.

Teaching resources can be adapted to different pathways and difficulty levels. Each adaptation needs explicit prerequisites, learning objectives, tasks and expected outputs; the domain itself has no fixed difficulty.

## Applied research and design opportunities

- Develop and evaluate a soil, water or habitat intervention.
- Compare outcomes across sites while preserving differences in context and practice.
