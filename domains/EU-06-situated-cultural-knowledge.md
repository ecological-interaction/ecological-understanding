# EU-06 — Situated & Cultural Knowledge

This domain investigates knowledge grounded in lived experience, practice, cultural expression and relationships with place. It supports careful engagement with oral histories, craft, local observation and material culture, respecting the authority, consent and conditions under which knowledge is shared.

This foundational page identifies opportunities for research and resource development. The activities below are proposals, not a claim that completed modules already exist. Research, Methods & Tools, Education & Programmes and Applied Practice are all supported within this domain.

## Scope and principal subjects

- Experiential, tacit, local, traditional and situated technical knowledge
- Oral histories, stories, historic maps, images and material culture
- Participatory inquiry, interpretation, positionality and translation
- Georeferencing, time attribution, consent, access conditions and stewardship

## Relevant research questions

- How is ecological knowledge transmitted through practices, stories and artefacts?
- What is lost when situated knowledge is reduced to a spatial record or database field?
- Who can authorise documentation, interpretation and reuse, and how can those permissions change?

## Potential methods and how-to components

- Prepare an interview and consent protocol with contributors.
- Record an artefact or oral account with provenance, time and access conditions.
- Compare historical and contemporary accounts of a landscape without forcing agreement.

## Teaching module opportunities

- Situated knowledge, evidence and relationships with place.
- Documenting cultural and practical knowledge through collaborative inquiry.

Teaching resources can be adapted to different pathways and difficulty levels. Each adaptation needs explicit prerequisites, learning objectives, tasks and expected outputs; the domain itself has no fixed difficulty.

## Applied research and design opportunities

- Develop a design brief with local practitioners and knowledge holders.
- Investigate changes in land-management practices through attributed accounts and material evidence.
